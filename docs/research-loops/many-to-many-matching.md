## What each association means

- `reviewLinks` records explicit task/source associations, including the `task_candidate` or `partial_task` relationship, rationale and limitations. A cited demonstration or partial task does not establish delivery or suitability for a complete customer workflow.
- `reviewIds` also includes constrained legacy task and workflow associations. Its presence alone does not mean a primary source documents the exact task. Humanoids do not inherit blanket family or workflow matches.
- `platformMatches` is separate. Source-backed movement and manipulation properties overlap recorded job requirements, making the robot a conditional integration option. Its source IDs, lifecycle, tooling requirements and limitations remain visible. Adding an option here does not create task evidence in `reviewLinks` or `reviewIds`.

`candidateReviewIds` combines and deduplicates task-linked and potential options. Each job may have several robots; each robot may appear for several jobs. Filled map dots and the Task evidence only filter require explicit `reviewLinks`. Older related-work leads and potential options remain visible when that checkbox is off. Users can also filter by robot type without changing the evidence distinction.

Potential matching requires recorded movement and hand-work requirements and compatible capability entries. It respects explicit task/family restrictions, excludes historical platforms from new potential matches, and retains access and optional-tooling conditions. Published handling limits can exclude an overweight job; missing weight, loaded reach, perception, fixtures and cycle-time evidence remain unresolved. Force-controlled handling research keeps its task-specific review but does not receive generic platform suggestions: dexterous hands alone do not establish joint torque feedback or suitable controller access.

## Meaningful map axes

The default **Job requirements** view places jobs by **Movement needed** (at a station; travel on level floors; steps or uneven terrain) and **Hand work needed** (no arm; simple grip and transfer; two-arm coordination; dexterous hand or tool work). Users can choose or swap these axes. They describe editorial assumptions about the proposed job, not measured robot ability, cost or readiness. Unknown requirements have an explicit category. Small offsets within a category keep individual jobs selectable and carry no additional meaning.

The optional **Similar tasks** view uses local similarity projection inside work clusters. It has no meaningful global numerical axes. Neither layout proves a candidate can perform the task.

## Audit outputs and limitations

The audit reads local records and writes JSON only under `.out/research-coverage`; it does not mutate the database or refresh sources. `summary.json` separates retained research from `publicDiscovery` and contains metric definitions. The retained collection currently has 236 opportunities and 83 configuration reviews, including archived classes.

The existing `opportunitiesWithCandidates` key now counts the union of task-linked and potential options. `opportunitiesWithTaskLinkedReviews` preserves the previous review-ID-only calculation. In `configurations.json`, the existing `tasks` field retains that earlier meaning; `sourceLinkedTasks`, `relatedWorkTasks`, `potentialTasks` and `candidateTasks` expose the distinctions. `unmatched-tasks.json` lists jobs with no options of either kind; `task-evidence-gaps.json` contains every job without explicit source links, including jobs that have only related-work or conditional options. There are 15 such jobs in the current public collection.

These counts describe recorded coverage. They do not establish automation accuracy, safety, successful deployment, stock, delivery or German availability. The broader catalogue inventory in the audit comes from its previously stored snapshot and is not reverified by this command.
